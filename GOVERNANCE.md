# LegacyGuard Governance

Status: Transitional governance for the pre-pilot phase

## Mission lock

LegacyGuard exists to reduce uncertainty for families by helping people
organize continuity information while preserving privacy, agency, dignity, and
human decision-making. Growth, funding, convenience, and contributor preference
do not override that mission.

## Current model

LegacyGuard is founder-led while it establishes its license, safety gates,
contributor community, and operating model. Material decisions must record the
problem, evidence, affected people, risks, alternatives, decision owner, review
conditions, and date.

## Feedback and contribution intake

Issues and Discussions may be used for ordinary, non-sensitive feedback.
Vulnerability reports must use a verified private route; public issues are not
an intake channel for security details or sensitive evidence.

Unsolicited code contributions and pull requests are not accepted. A pull
request may be submitted only when the project owner has specifically requested
or authorized it in writing. Opening an issue or discussion does not transfer
ownership or grant a license to incorporate submitted material. Before
accepting outside copyrightable work, the owner must establish and publish the
contribution and licensing terms. No DCO or CLA has been selected.

The repository's AGPL-3.0 license is present, but the owner must still resolve
provenance and authority to publish the existing project materials. This
policy does not make a legal ownership determination.

## Reserved decisions

During this phase, the founder must explicitly approve:

- license and contribution terms;
- repository visibility;
- collection or use of real personal information;
- pilot or production launch;
- material privacy-policy changes;
- funding arrangements that create influence or conflicts;
- appointment or removal of maintainers;
- transfer of trademarks, domains, infrastructure, or project control; and
- project shutdown or succession.

Changes to authentication, authorization, encryption, discovery, sharing,
recovery, deletion, trusted-contact behavior, or consequential automation also
require a written design record and security/privacy review.

## Maintainers and sponsors

Maintainers are appointed for sustained judgment, reliability, respectful
participation, and care for the mission—not merely code volume. They must use
least privilege, protect private reports, disclose conflicts, and recuse when
impartial review is not possible.

Sponsors may suggest priorities through documented channels. Sponsorship does
not purchase user information, privileged vulnerability treatment, hidden
roadmap control, weakened safeguards, or veto power.

## Continuity and evolution

Before public operation, at least two trusted people must be able to preserve or
safely suspend critical infrastructure without sharing daily credentials.
Domains, source hosting, deployment, secrets, backups, financial administration,
and succession authority must be documented and tested.

This model will be reviewed after the first three independent maintainers, the
first external funding milestone, and the first pilot. The intended direction
is accountable shared stewardship without weakening the mission lock.
